          Reference-based vs referenceless — the axis that decides your cost

          Reference-basedReferenceless
          Compares againstexpected_output, expected_tools, a gold trajectoryInternal consistency, or an LLM judging against a rubric
          Someone must writeThe reference, per case, and keep it currentNothing
          Scales to production trafficNo — curated test sets onlyYes, at any volume
          Precision of the signalHigh, and repeatableLower, and dependent on judge quality
          Belongs inDevelopment and CI, as a hard pass/fail barProduction monitoring
          Examples hereP@K · R@K · nDCG · Contextual Precision/Recall · Answer Correctness · ToolCorrectnessMetricContextual Relevancy · Faithfulness · Answer Relevancy · ArgumentCorrectnessMetric · TaskCompletionMetric
          Mature setups run both, at different points in the lifecycle — not as a choice between them. Reference-based gates the deploy; referenceless watches what happens after it.

          Where LLM-as-judge actually breaks

          Failure modeWhat it looks likeWhat to do about it
          Weak judge modelNoisy scores that don't track human judgementThe judge must be at least as capable as the task requires, sometimes more than the system it grades
          Non-determinismTwo runs on identical input disagree, especially near the thresholdTreat metric.score as a noisy estimate; set thresholds away from where scores cluster
          Criteria sensitivityA vague GEval criteria string produces a vague judgeWrite evaluation_steps explicitly; the rubric's specificity is the judgement's specificity
          Cost compounding5 metrics × 500 cases = 2,500+ judge calls per runDeterministic checks first; judge only what arithmetic can't decide
          Gamed by fluencyA confident, well-written sentence scores well regardless of truthEnd-state verification — read the system of record, not the agent's summary
