RAG & Retrieval Interview Drill

RAG means Retrieval-Augmented Generation: before you ask a model a question, you go find the relevant documents and paste them into the prompt. That's the whole idea — everything here is about doing it well. Built from eight notebooks in 02_RAG_and_Retrieval. Try to recall before you reveal; the click is the point.

Words you need first

EmbeddingA list of numbers standing for a piece of text. Similar meanings give similar numbers.
ChunkA small piece of a document. You split because whole documents search badly.
Vector storeA database built to search lists of numbers fast. Chroma, FAISS, Pinecone.
RetrieverThe thing that takes a question and hands back the most relevant chunks.
Top-kHow many chunks you fetch. k=3 means "give me the best 3".
Cosine similarityA score from -1 to 1 for how close two embeddings are in meaning. Higher is closer.
BM25An older keyword-matching algorithm. Great at exact words, which embeddings are bad at.
RerankerA second, smarter model that reorders your top chunks so the best one is first.
recall@kAcross your test questions, how often was the correct chunk in the top k?
p95 latency95% of requests finish faster than this. The number users actually feel.
ACLAccess Control List — the rules for which user may see which document.
MMRMaximal Marginal Relevance. Re-ranks for variety, so you don't get three copies of one idea.

Section 08 — Mock design

Enterprise research assistant, in real time

The prompt Design a real-time research assistant for an enterprise. It answers questions over 500k internal documents, streams responses, supports multi-turn follow-ups, must cite sources, and must never surface a document the asking user cannot access. Target: first token under 1 second, p95 complete answer under 8 seconds. Walk me through it. 
flowchart TD
  U["User question + identity"] --> R["Retrieval service"]
  R --> F["ACL pre-filter by user groups"]
  F --> H["Hybrid search: BM25 + vectors"]
  H --> K["Fetch about 30 candidates"]
  K --> RR["Rerank to top 5"]
  RR --> P["Build prompt"]
  M[("Session memory in Redis")] --> P
  P --> L["Model, streaming"]
  L --> A["Answer with citations"]
  L --> T["Trace: run id, chunk ids, cost"]
One shape a strong answer converges on

A strong answer opens by clarifying — query volume, document churn, tenancy model, and what "cite" actually means — because all four change the design. Then: chunk near 500 tokens with 15% overlap and tune against an eval set; stable chunk IDs so reingestion upserts rather than duplicates; the ACL as a required pre-filter argument in a retrieval service, never a prompt instruction; hybrid BM25 plus dense because internal corpora are full of identifiers embeddings blur; fetch ~30, rerank to 5. Say out loud that the 1-second target is time to first token, not completion, and that you get there by streaming, overlapping retrieval with history fetch, and routing simple lookups to a small model. Session history in Redis, not in process, because replicas and deploys. When retrieval returns nothing above threshold, return a distinct response type so the UI and the metrics both see it. Traces keyed by run and session ID recording retrieved chunk IDs — that one field turns "why did it say that" from a five-hour question into a five-minute one.

Section 09 — Self-check

Rapid fire

Answer out loud, then click. If you need more than five seconds, it goes back on the pile.

Explain to a skeptical staff engineer

  • Why hybrid search is worth running two indexes.
  • Why you would refuse to put access control in the system prompt.
  • Why your session memory design fails on a second replica, and what you'd change.
  • Why multi-agent is probably the wrong answer to their problem.
  • Why "the answers look better" is not evidence.
