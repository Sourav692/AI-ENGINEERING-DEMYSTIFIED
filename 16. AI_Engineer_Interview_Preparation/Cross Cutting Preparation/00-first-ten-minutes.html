The First Ten Minutes

What to ask before you touch the whiteboard — so the interviewer sees an FDE, not a solver reaching for the pen.

Briefing · pre-whiteboard checklist 5 areas · ask 3–4

Interviewers aren't testing whether you can design a system — they're testing whether you'd waste three months building the wrong one. Ask before you architect. Pick a few questions per area below, ask them conversationally, and let the answers visibly steer your design out loud. Applies the same way whether the problem is a RAG platform, an agent platform, or a delivery process.

01

Who's the customer, really

  • Is this for one enterprise customer, or a platform serving many tenants?
  • What decision or workflow does the answer actually feed into?
  • Who are the end users — internal employees, external customers, or both? Technical or not?

Why it mattersOne answer here decides your entire isolation model — and, for an agent platform, the entire authoring surface — before a single architecture choice.

02

Data & system reality

  • What shape is the underlying data or the systems being integrated with today?
  • Is it static, or does it change — and how fast?
  • Who owns data/access quality, and can we get it before day one?

Why it mattersMost demos assume clean, structured input. Real deployments never are.

03

Access control & blast radius

  • Does every user/tenant have uniform access, or does visibility vary by group, role, or region?
  • Is the system read-only, or can it take real actions — and what costs more, a wrong action or an over-cautious refusal?

Why it mattersDecides how much of the design has to be permission-aware and guardrailed, or can stay simple.

04

Scale & SLAs

  • Roughly how many tenants/users/items, and what volume of requests?
  • What latency is acceptable — is that p50, or p95/p99?

Why it mattersTen of something and ten million are different systems, not different sizes of one.

05

Definition of done

  • How will we know if this is working — is quality or accuracy even measured?
  • Is there a fallback if it's wrong or down — does it escalate to a human?

Why it mattersOffering to own evaluation is the strongest FDE signal in the room.

Why this reads as FDE mindset

Don't fire all twelve questions. Ask three or four, then visibly let the answers change the design, out loud — "okay, if it's multi-tenant with per-user visibility, that changes how I'd structure this, let me start there." The adaptation is the signal, not the question count.
