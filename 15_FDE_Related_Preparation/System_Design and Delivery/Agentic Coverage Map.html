Agentic Coverage Map

Two interview walkthroughs — an Enterprise AI Assistant wired into 100+ internal apps, and a Customer Support AI Assistant built on RAG, tools, and memory — read side by side against the common vocabulary of agentic system design.

Verdict

Yes. Between the two, they cover nearly every concept an agentic-system-design interview expects. Neither one alone is complete — the Enterprise Assistant goes deep on integration methods and tool-discovery at scale but never mentions RAG, memory, or human approval; the Customer Support Assistant covers the full agent loop (RAG, memory, human-in-the-loop, model routing) but doesn't revisit the tool-registry scaling problem. Together they're complementary, not redundant.

What neither one covers

The gaps, if you're using these two as full interview prep for agentic system design.

Guardrails & prompt-injection defense

Neither transcript discusses treating retrieved documents or tool output as untrusted input, or validating model output before it triggers an action.

Prompt management / versioning

System prompts are implied but never treated as versioned, tested artifacts the way the rest of the stack is.

Disaster recovery & chaos testing

Multi-region deployment is covered for latency and residency, but neither walks through what happens when a region — or the LLM provider itself — goes down.

Explicit capacity estimation

Both state a target (1M or 10M conversations/day; 50K–200K employees) but neither derives RPS, storage, or bandwidth from it the way a general system-design rubric would.
