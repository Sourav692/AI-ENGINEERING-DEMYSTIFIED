# Cross Team Collaboration

*System Design & Delivery Interviews — Reference*

The interview question: **"Tell me about a time when you led a cross-team data/AI project involving multiple stakeholders. How did you ensure alignment, ownership, and effective communication?"** The hard part of a cross-team AI platform is rarely the technology — it's aligning teams with different priorities and tightly coupled dependencies.

`5 stakeholder groups` · `RACI ownership` · `tiered communication` · `phased delivery`

---

## 1 · The interview question

**Alignment, ownership, and communication — not just delivery.**

> "Tell me about a time when you led a cross-team data/AI project involving multiple stakeholders. How did you ensure alignment, ownership, and effective communication?"

A strong example: a global data and AI platform delivered to generate seller and executive insights. The real challenge wasn't just building the platform — it was aligning multiple teams with different priorities and dependencies. This framing is deliberate: naming the actual difficulty up front (not the technology) is what signals senior-level thinking before a single detail is given.

---

## 2 · Stakeholders & complexity

**Five groups, tightly coupled dependencies.**

This initiative involved software teams, a data engineering team, AI/ML engineers, a project manager, and business stakeholders — each with a different stake in the outcome and a different definition of "done." The key challenge: different teams had conflicting priorities, and dependencies were tightly coupled, increasing delivery risk.

```
                Software Teams
              (upstream apps & APIs)
                       │
Business Stakeholders  │  Data Engineering
  (success metrics) ───┼─── (ingestion & lake)
                Global Data & AI
                    Platform
                       │
  Project Manager      │      AI/ML Engineers
(timelines & coord.) ──┴── (forecasting models)
```

Five stakeholder groups, one platform: software teams own upstream application data and APIs, the DE team builds ingestion and lake pipelines, AI/ML engineers develop forecasting and insight models, the PM manages timelines and cross-team coordination, and business stakeholders define success metrics and consume outputs.

---

## 3 · Establishing clear ownership (RACI)

**Removing ambiguity before it causes delay.**

To remove ambiguity, a RACI matrix was implemented across all major workstreams — who is Responsible for doing the work, Accountable for the outcome, Consulted before decisions, and Informed after them.

| Workstream | Responsible | Accountable | Consulted | Informed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data Ingestion | DE Team | Data Platform Lead | Software Teams | PM, Business |
| Data Modeling (Lake) | DE Team | Data Platform Lead | AI Engineers | PM |
| ML Forecasting Models | AI Engineers | ML Lead | DE Team, Business | PM |
| API / Data Access | Software Teams | Engineering Manager | DE Team | PM, Business |
| Dashboards & Insights | BI / AI Team | Product Owner | Business Stakeholders | PM |
| Program Delivery | Project Manager | Program Sponsor | All Teams | Leadership |

This helped eliminate ownership confusion, speed up decision-making, and ensure accountability at every stage — a workstream with an unclear owner is where cross-team projects quietly stall.

## 4 · Communication strategy

**Structured and outcome-driven, not more meetings.**

The focus was structured and outcome-driven communication, rather than increasing meeting load. A tiered communication model matched cadence to altitude: daily async updates for progress and blockers, a weekly cross-team sync for dependencies and decisions, and bi-weekly executive updates for impact and risk.

```
📌 Daily Async Updates — shared tracker: progress, risks, blockers
                │
📌 Weekly Cross-Team Sync — focused only on dependencies and decisions
                │
📌 Bi-Weekly Executive Updates — impact, risks, and progress

Higher frequency, more detail  →  lower frequency, more altitude
```

Each tier answers a different question at a different frequency — the daily tracker keeps peers unblocked, the weekly sync resolves cross-team dependencies, and the bi-weekly update keeps leadership informed without pulling them into the details.

**🚫 No status-only meetings** — Every interaction must unblock or decide something — a meeting that only reports status is a written update that got a calendar invite.

**📍 Single source of truth** — One centralized tracking system — not five spreadsheets and a Slack thread each claiming to be current.

**✍️ Write-first culture** — Decisions documented before discussion, so meetings resolve disagreements instead of generating the first draft of them.

**⚡ Fast escalation** — Blockers raised within 24 hours — a dependency that sits unescalated for a week is how tightly coupled workstreams cascade into delay.

**Bridging tech and business:** technical issues were translated into business impact — "data delay affects forecast accuracy and seller decisions," not "the ingestion job failed." The customer was kept continuously engaged, not just briefed at delivery.

## 5 · Conflict resolution

**Speed vs. reliability, resolved with contracts and phasing.**

A key conflict emerged: software teams prioritized speed of feature delivery, while DE and AI teams needed stable, high-quality data. Neither priority was wrong — they were just optimizing for different stages of the same pipeline. The resolution introduced data contracts and delivered in two phases, balancing speed and reliability instead of forcing one team to simply lose the argument.

```
Software Teams              DE / AI Teams
want: speed          need: stable, high-quality data
        └───────────┬───────────┘
                Data Contracts
                     │
              Phase 1: Reliable core dataset
                     │
              Phase 2: Advanced features
```

Data contracts turned an ownership argument into an interface agreement: phase one shipped a reliable core dataset, and phase two layered advanced features on top of it once the foundation was stable.

---

## 6 · Execution & delivery

**RACI and cadence, put into practice.**

- Workstreams aligned to RACI ownership, so every deliverable had exactly one accountable owner.
- Centralized tracking of dependencies kept the tightly coupled workstreams visible to everyone, not just the PM.
- Regular integration checkpoints ensured alignment before divergence became expensive to unwind.

---

## 7 · Outcome

**A platform, and a repeatable way of working.**

`Better demand forecasting` `Reduced stockouts and wastage` `Repeatable collaboration model for future programs`

Successfully delivered a scalable global data and AI platform — and, arguably more valuable long-term, created a repeatable collaboration model that other cross-team programs could reuse without rebuilding the RACI and communication structure from scratch.

---

## 8 · Key learnings

**What actually moved the needle.**

- Clear ownership via RACI significantly improves execution speed.
- Communication must be intentional and structured, not frequent — more meetings is not the same as more alignment.
- Early alignment across teams prevents late-stage risks that are far more expensive to resolve after integration.
